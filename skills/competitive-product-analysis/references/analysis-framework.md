# Analysis Framework

## Three-Ring Pool

1. Direct task competitors solve the same user task for a similar audience.
2. Adjacent solutions solve the task through another device, service, or workflow.
3. Analogous experiences reveal useful interaction, trust, habit, or business-model patterns without competing directly.

The three rings prevent category labels from hiding substitutes and design analogies.

## Evidence, Hypothesis, Decision

- `E — Evidence`: a source-supported observation with date and market context.
- `H — Hypothesis`: an interpretation that can be tested or falsified.
- `D — Decision`: a recommendation based on evidence, constraints, and priorities.

Do not allow a score, chart, or polished layout to erase these distinctions.

## Interaction-Heavy Experience Dimensions

Use these for hardware, software workflows, or services where moment-to-moment interaction is part of the decision. They are not universal defaults; select the primary domain dimensions from `dimension-packs.md` first.

- Task object: what job or information object the product acts on.
- State model: how the user understands idle, listening, processing, ready, failed, and interrupted states.
- Attention mechanism: whether the product demands eyes, hands, voice, or continuous monitoring.
- Feedback channel: display, audio, light, haptics, movement, or a companion device.
- Intervention mode: view, confirm, correct, postpone, cancel, undo, or delegate.
- Cross-device ability: how work moves among hardware, phone, desktop, cloud, and third-party systems.
- Safety and reversibility: consent, visibility, undo, export, offline fallback, and failure recovery.

Add or replace dimensions when the decision depends more on price, distribution, manufacturability, ecosystem access, compliance, or unit economics.

## Scoring

- Define what high and low mean for every scored dimension.
- Weight dimensions only after the decision question is written.
- Keep the evidence and rationale beside the score.
- Use `unknown` instead of a neutral score when evidence is missing.
- Run a sensitivity check when small weight changes reverse the recommendation.

## Opportunity Hypothesis Template

- Target user and scenario.
- Unmet task or harmful workaround.
- Evidence and observed pattern.
- Proposed product or interaction mechanism.
- Principles that cannot be sacrificed.
- Lowest-cost prototype or test.
- Main technical, behavioral, business, or regulatory risk.
- Falsification condition.

Prefer three to five strong hypotheses over a long feature backlog.
